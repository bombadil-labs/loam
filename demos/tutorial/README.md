# The tutorial — learn Loam by growing one (§48)

Deployed at <https://bombadil-labs.github.io/loam/tutorial.html> (the site root is the landing
page, `demos/site/`) by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` on every push to main. No signup, no
server, nothing installed: the page boots a real governed store in the visitor's browser and
teaches by doing.

The tutorial is TWO things kept apart on purpose — a player and an arc.

- **`player.mjs`** — the ENGINE, UI-free. Progress is deltas: entering a lesson, banking a step,
  answering a quiz, taking a checkpoint and planting a glossary term all land as signed claims
  in the student's own store under a `tutorial.*` vocabulary, and every reading here recomputes
  from the ground. There is no parallel state machine, so a reload cannot disagree with the
  store. It also owns the CHECKPOINTS (`loam:tutorial-ckpt:<lesson>`, one per lesson boundary,
  superseded in place) and their SWEEP: an erasure destroys every checkpoint that holds the
  erased bytes, names them, and keeps the ones that cannot — a copy holds the bytes, so a right
  to be forgotten that spares the undo button is not one.
- **`lessons.mjs`** — the ARC as data: **"A Store of Your Own"**, fifteen lessons in five acts
  (T227). Each lesson is `{id, role, title, copy, terms, quiz?, steps, check}` and each step is
  `{id, label, have, want, how, run, observe}`: the three-way framing (what we have / what we
  want / how we get there) and TWO machine-checkable observables, one over the page and one over
  the store. A step banks only when both hold, so a `run` that quietly stops working is refused
  by name rather than ticked off.

  The story is a film diary. The spine is the **viewing** — a date, a film, a rating — because a
  rewatch is a second true thing rather than a contradiction, and the other voice is **Rae**, who
  is wrong about everything on your couch. Act I makes a store and writes in it; Act II changes
  its mind, twice, and reads the past; Act III hands Rae a pen and discovers that "whose word
  wins" is the reader's question; Act IV meets a stranger and takes a pen back; Act V forgets
  something on purpose and walks out with the rest.

  Two things about it are mechanical rather than editorial. **The arc exports its own glossary**
  (`TERMS`, twenty-one words in the order a student meets them, each with the plain-words meaning
  it plants as a claim), and `test/site/arc.test.ts` scans every sentence of lesson copy for a
  word used before the lesson that introduces it. And **every claim the arc writes is hand-signed
  at `ctx.ts()`** rather than through the mutation door: `gateway.nextTimestamp()` and `ctx.ts()`
  are two monotonic counters, and interleaving them would leave "whose word is latest" decided by
  a millisecond race — a coin flip on every latest-wins read in Act III.
- **`app.mjs` / `index.html` / `style.css`** — the theater: zero framework, the store is the
  state and the page is a subscriber. One pending step at a time, a progress rail with revert,
  the quiz card, the glossary pane, the introspection drawer, and the Ground pane — which
  filters the tutorial's own records out by default, behind a visible toggle.
- **`instruments.mjs`** — the panes as tools: the delta classifier (badges, including
  `tutorial`), the Ground renderer and its filter, and the View browser.
- **`packets/`** — the bundled world (the circle, the adversary, the dialect): committed data,
  regenerated byte-identically by `scripts/gen-packets.mjs` (`--check` gates drift in CI). **No
  lesson reads them today.** Act IV's stranger builds their own file inside the lesson — signed
  at run time, wired through `parseOffer` and the real federation door — because the arc needs a
  file that claims to be Rae, and none of the three packets is that. They stay committed and
  gated because a later arc (the advanced sequel, T225) is where the circle and the dialect
  belong.

**Two rules the page keeps, both load-bearing.** It never calls `window.confirm`: a native
dialog blocks the page's own script until a human answers, so it is a destructive path no rail
can drive — revert and start-over confirm with controls in the page. And revert RELOADS after
re-seeding the rows, because this module captures the gateway once and the View pane holds a
live subscription; reloading makes "the panes re-render from the restored ground" true by
construction.

**The lesson ROLES are a contract**, not decoration: `test/browser/tutorial.test.ts` freezes at
its landing and finds what it exercises by role. An arc that renames or drops one breaks that
suite by design. What each role must actually DO:

- `opening` — the first lesson; boot has already happened.
- `reveal` — plants at least one glossary term from INSIDE a step, not from the lesson's
  `terms` (which are planted on entry). The where-does-this-live payoff needs the student to
  have done something for it.
- `erasure-finale` — erases a record that landed AFTER at least one checkpoint boundary. One
  blob is then holding those bytes when the sweep runs, and an earlier blob is not: the frozen
  suite asserts both sides. Erasing something the same lesson created leaves the sweep nothing
  to destroy; erasing a lesson-1 record leaves it no bystander to spare. Either turns the frozen
  file red with no bug behind it. In this arc Rae writes the condemned note in lesson 13 and
  lesson 14 erases it, so twelve boundaries predate it and exactly one holds it.

**THE SATISFIABILITY RULE**, which governs every `observe.page` and is checked mechanically
(`test/site/arc.test.ts`, "every step's page observable roots at an element the SHELL declares"):

> A step's `observe.page.selector` must root at an element declared in `index.html` whose content
> renders from the STORE.

Two halves, both learned from a trap this arc walked into:

- **(a) STATE, NOT EVENT.** `seePage` must hold in every store state where the step's store
  predicate holds — including after a bare reload, and when `run` finds its work already done. An
  observable that is true only in the instant after its own run is a trap on any irreversible
  step: the act cannot be repeated, so the step can never be completed.
- **(b) NO `ui.*` DEPENDENCE.** No selector may name an element that exists only while some page
  field is set. The tell is a selector absent from `index.html`.

**Three rules the frozen suite pins about the finale:** it must be FULLY PASSABLE with every
checkpoint blob deleted — so no finale step may observe the revert rail *shrinking*, since there
may be nothing to shrink; the sweep notice is the witness, and it speaks for every forgetting,
destroyed or not. ONE PRESS PER FINALE STEP; a two-stage act belongs to two steps. And two copy
pins are frozen: a finished quiz card's button says **"done"**, and a sweep that found nothing says
**"there was nothing to destroy"**.

**Four more things the arc owes the player**, each with a rail that fails without it: every
lesson has at least one step whose store predicate is FALSE before it runs (a lesson of pure
look-steps teaches a click and proves nothing); every step carries both observables; at least
one lesson declares a quiz; and STEP IDS ARE DURABLE — a banked step is a claim naming the
step by id, so renaming one in a later version silently un-banks it for every student who
already did it.

**And the ENTITY ids are the same promise.** A viewing is a claim filed at `viewing:arrival`; a
glossary word is a claim at `tutorial:term:<word>`. Rename one and the store on somebody's laptop
still points at the old name — their diary entry becomes an orphan nothing reads, exactly as a
renamed step un-banks itself. `test/site/arc.test.ts` pins every one of them literally ("the arc's
names are durable"), because nothing else could: renaming an entity consistently changes no
behaviour any other rail can see, and mutation testing walked straight through it.

Build locally: `node scripts/build-site.mjs` then `node scripts/serve-site.mjs` →
<http://127.0.0.1:4173/>. (`--out <dir>` builds elsewhere; the browser rail uses it so two
suites never share one output directory.)

The rails: `test/site/arc.test.ts` walks all fifteen lessons headlessly and asserts every step's
STORE observable, the progress claims per kind against the arc's own totals, the glossary trail
in the manifest's order, the vocabulary scan (green on the arc, red on a term planted early), the
checkpoints and the sweep; `test/browser/tutorial.test.ts` drives the real page in a real
Chrome — stepping, the red probe, revert, resume with every non-store key deleted, the quiz, the
Ground filter, the glossary reveal, the drawer, and the sweep at the bytes.

**One asymmetry, on purpose.** The finale's own sweep observable is ONE-SIDED — it may not
require a surviving checkpoint, because the frozen suite requires that lesson to be passable
with every blob deleted, and a student whose quota refused every checkpoint must not be stranded
on the last lesson. The other side (a bystander blob is SPARED) is asserted in `arc.test.ts`,
which owns the fixture and can guarantee a bystander exists to be spared.
