  [33mMEDIUM  [39m [35m[other][39m [1mDefault posture is the expensive, hard-to-reverse 'wall'; an omitted posture silently allocates a separate store[22m  [2mconf 0.48[22m
    [36m.adlc/specs/27-container-lifting.md:38-40[39m
    `trust` is REQUIRED but `posture` defaults to `wall`. A curated declaration that omits posture becomes a curated WALL — 'your OWN separate store' paying real byte copies — rather than the zero-copy property scope. Because posture is immutable and 'property→wall is unachievable in place, wall→property is the escalation that must pass the root's admission,' an accidental defaulted wall cannot be corrected by re-declaration; it forces a root-admission escalation or a brand-new container. The cheap, common case (a zero-copy curated scope) requires explicit posture while the expensive, near-irreversible case is what you get by forgetting a field. No criterion flags the cost/irreversibility of the defaulted posture.
    [33m✗ failure:[39m Operator intends a curated property container (flippable exclusion, zero copies) and declares `{trust: curated}` expecting the light-weight scope, omitting posture. The door accepts it as a curated WALL, allocates a separate backend, and begins paying byte copies; it also becomes subject to the erasure completeness guard. The operator cannot re-declare to property (immutable, refuses) and must run a root-admission escalation or mint a new container to undo an accidental allocation.
    [32m→ fix:[39m Either make posture REQUIRED on the permanent surface (same 'explicitness over defaults' argument already used to require `trust`), or explicitly justify why the expensive+immutable posture is the safe default and add a criterion asserting the operator-visible consequence (separate store allocated) of an omitted posture.
