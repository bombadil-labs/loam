# A Store of Your Own — the v3 arc script (draft 2, Myk-approved 2026-08-22)

Companion to `.adlc/specs/48-tutorial-v3.md` (the working spec, merged #450). This is the
CONTENT contract: fifteen lessons in five acts. The spec's laws govern every lesson: features
appear only when the story needs them; no term before its plain introduction, each landing a
glossary delta in the student's store; erasure is the finale and the checkpoint sweep is the
lesson; every step is one action ending in a page observable AND a store observable.

**The frame.** The student is *you*, second person. The other voice is **Rae** — the person you
share a couch and violently incompatible film takes with. The engine: you want a record of what
you watched, when, and what you honestly thought at the time; Rae keeps having opinions about
your opinions; no app lets both truths coexist. The diary's spine is the **viewing** (date,
film, rating) — a rewatch is a second true fact, never a contradiction.

Step notation: **do** → *see* (page observable) / *holds* (store observable). These become the
`observe` predicates at build. Terms per lesson are the glossary deltas the lesson plants.
Quizzes close acts — five total, optional, wrong answers linking the teaching step.

## Act I — A place of your own

**01 · The keys are yours.** Boot already happened; the page says in plain words: this is a
store, it keeps records, you hold the only key. Steps: look at the constitution record in the
Ground (see: the badge and your key / holds: the operator claim, signed this session); press
"prove it's mine" (see: verification sentence / holds: signature verifies against your public
key); **download your key — "keep it somewhere safe!"** (see: the file lands, one warning /
holds: nothing new — the seed never enters the ground, and that absence is the closing
sentence). Terms: store, record, key, operator.

**02 · Say what a viewing is.** You watched Arrival last night; a store keeps records, not
vibes, so first say what the thing IS: a viewing — date, film, rating — plus one house rule:
when viewings disagree about a film, your latest word wins. That description is a lens.
Step: register the Viewing lens (see: "Viewing" under Lenses, query pane offers the fields /
holds: the registration record, readable back as data). Terms: lens, shape.

**03 · Last night: Arrival, a 9.** The entry becomes a signed claim; answers are assembled
from claims at the moment you ask. Steps: log the viewing (see: three panes move at once, the
claim badge, your signature / holds: a viewing claim under your key); add the note "made me cry
on a Tuesday" (see: note in diary AND a second Ground record — nothing edited, something added /
holds: two claims, both live). Terms: claim, signature.

*Quiz I:* where do answers come from; what did the page keep after the key download (nothing —
the point); what did adding the note NOT do.

## Act II — Changing your mind

**04 · The rewatch.** Two weeks later: honestly, a 7. Not a dilemma — you watched it twice, the
diary holds two viewings because both happened; "Arrival's rating" resolves to your latest
word with the 9 still true underneath. Steps: log the rewatch (see: two dated entries, rating
resolves 7 / holds: two viewing claims, resolution picks the later); open the "everything"
toggle (see: the conversation your opinions have been having / holds: the ground, the full
claim set the lens reads). Terms: ground, view.

**05 · Unsaying.** Your Tenet viewing carries the note "understood it completely." You did not.
Nobody does. Strike it — a new record saying "I take that back": gone from every reading,
struck-but-present in the ground. The diary proves you outgrew it. Step: strike the note (see:
note gone from diary; a "strike" record pointing at the old claim, now dimmed, not deleted /
holds: the negation, and the struck claim at rest). Terms: strike.

**06 · The diary as of last month.** What did you think of Arrival BEFORE the rewatch changed
your mind — not what you remember thinking, what you said at the time? Same question, one extra
word: as of last month. The 9 is back; so is the struck Tenet note, because last month you
still believed it. Steps: run the query with a moment set (see: 9, the note live, the banner
naming the moment / holds: as-of read over only the claims that existed then); drag the moment
back to now (see: the present reassembles — nothing restored because nothing lost / holds: the
live read unchanged). Terms: moment.

*Quiz II:* what did striking remove from the ground (nothing); why does the diary keep both
viewings; why is the struck note visible as of last month.

## Act III — Other voices

**07 · Rae gets a pen.** Rae's verdict on Arrival: a 4 ("it's slow"). Rae gets their own key;
everything Rae writes is signed as Rae. Steps: mint Rae's key, Rae logs their viewing (see:
Rae's entry with a different signature / holds: a viewing claim under Rae's key); try the
forgery trap — write AS Rae with your key (see: the page explains a signature can only be made
by the key that owns it / holds: no new claim). Terms: author.

**08 · Whose word wins?** What's Arrival rated in this house? The store refuses to have an
opinion — the reader chooses, three times in two minutes: latest-wins (4), mine-first (7, Rae's
noted beneath), keep-everything (both, attributed). Same ground, three true answers. The rule
is a policy, and it lives in the lens, not the platform. Steps: one read per policy, each with
its see/holds pinned to the resolution. Terms: policy.

**09 · My shelf, our shelf.** Both worlds permanently: My Diary (your word, for your future
self) and House Diary (the argument in full, for the fridge door). Two lenses, one ground,
nothing copied. Step: register the second lens (see: both shelves answering at once / holds:
two registrations resolving live over one ground). Terms: none (deepens lens, policy).

**10 · Movie night — the evolution lesson.** You and Rae start watching things TOGETHER; the
viewing shape has no way to say that. Evolve it: a new version of Viewing that carries guests.
THE CRUCIAL POINT, in both directions, each with its own step: the old lens still works — even
against viewings the new lens wrote — and old records need nothing to live in the new world.
Steps: register the new version (see: Viewing with two versions, the new one serving / holds:
both registrations, newer superseding); log movie night — Paddington 2, a 10, Rae as guest
(see: the entry with Rae beside it / holds: a claim carrying a guests pointer at Rae); read
tonight's entry through the OLD lens (see: rendered perfectly, no guests field, nothing broken,
one sentence saying why / holds: the same claim resolving under the old registration); read
your first entry through the NEW lens (see: guest-aware shape, guests simply empty / holds:
the old claim resolving under the new registration). Terms: version.

**11 · It was records all along — THE REVEAL.** The glossary you've been consulting lives in
YOUR store — every definition a claim planted as you learned it; your progress and quiz
answers too. The tutorial has no memory of its own; it has been reading yours. And the real
name: a record here is called a delta — you've been reading deltas since lesson one. Steps:
open the glossary's "where does this live?" control (see: each entry highlights its claim in
the Ground, timestamped to its lesson / holds: glossary claims under the tutorial vocabulary);
query your own progress like any data (see: your journey as query results / holds: progress
claims resolving through the same machinery). Terms: delta.

*Quiz III:* movie night through the old lens — what happens (renders; can't see guests); where
does the glossary live; one ground, two lenses — how many copies of the data.

## Act IV — Who gets in

**12 · A file claims to be Rae.** Someone sends "Rae's viewings" — Tenet, ten out of ten,
suspicious. Import it and something wonderful happens: nothing. The claims land (the store
doesn't burn mail) but they're signed by a stranger's key your readers don't trust, so they
bind nothing. Steps: import (see: claims in the Ground wearing an unknown signature; every
shelf unchanged / holds: the stranger's claims at rest, resolving into no view); flip the house
shelf to "everyone, attributed" (see: forgeries appear only when a reader invites strangers,
wearing their true key, never Rae's name / holds: the trust mask deciding per reader).
Terms: trust.

**13 · The cousin incident.** Rae's cousin logged fourteen Fast & Furious viewings through
Rae's pen. Revoke it: the door refuses that key from now on — but every claim it made keeps
its author, because history that can be quietly rewritten isn't history. Then the part other
systems get wrong twice: **Rae gets a fresh key on the spot.** Keys are cheap; STANDING is what
you govern. Steps: revoke (see: the next write refuses, naming why / holds: the revocation, no
new claim); check the cousin's fourteen (see: still there, attributed to the old pen, strikable
/ holds: authorship surviving revocation); mint Rae a fresh key and Rae logs a viewing (see:
Rae writing again, new signature distinct from the old / holds: a claim under the new key, the
old key still refused). Terms: grant, revoke.

*Quiz IV:* why is the forged file landing fine; who wrote the cousin's viewings after the
revocation; why is a fresh key better than "cleaning" the old one.

## Act V — Leaving, and forgetting

**14 · What never should have landed.** Rae pasted a friend's private message into a viewing
note — a real person's words, without their say-so. Striking isn't enough: struck claims stay
at rest, and these bytes shouldn't exist here at all. The operator-only door: erase. The bytes
leave; a receipt remains — that something was removed, by whom, when, why — never what. Then
the tutorial does the honest thing with its own machinery: **checkpoints from before the
erasure are destroyed on screen, each named, the reason stated** — a checkpoint is a copy, a
copy holds the bytes, and a right to be forgotten that spares your undo button is a lie. The
revert rail visibly shortens; that loss is the lesson. Steps: erase with a stated reason (see:
gone from every view AND the raw ground; the receipt; the diary untouched around the hole /
holds: bytes absent at the storage layer, bystanders resolving, the signed receipt); try to
re-import the erased bytes (see: the door refuses them by name / holds: no resurrection);
watch the checkpoint sweep (see: the rail shorten, each checkpoint named, the why / holds: no
checkpoint blob with the erased bytes, later checkpoints intact); read as of last week (see:
the past minus what was lawfully forgotten, with the confession footnote / holds: the as-of
read excluding the erased claim, annotated). Terms: erase, receipt.

*Quiz V:* what does the receipt remember and what can it never remember; why did the
checkpoints have to go; striking versus erasing — the difference, and who can do each.

**15 · The homecoming.** Export, two commands on your own machine, and the finale: laptop copy
and tab copy identical, hash for hash — proven, not promised. And the erasure traveled: the
note is gone there too, receipt intact, the diary whole — two years of viewings, two voices,
one evolution, one forgetting. What you carry out is exactly what the store stands behind,
including what it forgot. You've had the key since lesson one. Steps: export, pull, compare
(see: two content addresses, equal / holds: byte-identical stores); query the laptop copy for
the erased note (see: absence, and the receipt / holds: no erased bytes anywhere in the
export). Closing trailer: "Rae's getting a store of their own. That's the next journey."
(→ T225). Terms: none — the glossary is full.

## Production decisions (settled in workshop)

- 15 lessons, 5 acts, ~34 steps; the reveal at 11 of 15.
- Terms budget: 21 glossary deltas (the list is authoritative), in this order: store, record, key, operator, lens, shape,
  claim, signature, ground, view, strike, moment, author, policy, version, delta, trust, grant,
  revoke, erase, receipt. (delta is withheld until the reveal; schema/hyperschema/gather never
  appear — the glossary may carry a "deeper names" appendix.)
- Quizzes close acts, never lessons; wrong answers link the teaching step; always skippable.
- The checkpoint sweep is staged as loss (the rail shortens in real time), not apology.
- Cut from v2 without replacement: the Book schema, the animate store, the door-policy
  deep-dive. Containers and federation belong to the advanced sequel (T225).
